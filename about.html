DETAILED ABOUT ME

Building a Career in Healthcare and Law

Olivia Scott

I have always had an interest in careers that require problem solving and environments that are constantly evolving and shifting. However, I also wanted to have a career that helps and serves people. My time at the University of South Florida helped me narrow those interests towards a specific direction. I am graduating with a Bachelor of Science in Information Science with a concentration in Healthcare Informatics along with a minor in Communications Fall 2026. My interests have gradually shifted from solely how technology impacts healthcare into how it has set a precedent in regard to patient rights and advocacy. Along with how laws and regulations are shaping the systems forced to change due to technological advancements.

The culmination of these events is what solidified my long-term goals. Upon completing my undergraduate degree, I plan to continue into a graduate program in Healthcare Informatics. With a strong educational background in healthcare and technology, I plan to attend law school and focus on health law. My goal is to have a career utilizing the knowledge I have gained about the healthcare systems and its technology to support policy reform, patient advocacy and rights.

Throughout my coursework I have encountered databases, web development, information security, programming, and information organization. I have acquired experience in Microsoft Access, SQL, and multiple programming languages such as Java and C. Studying concepts such as electronic health records and clinical information systems has shown me that healthcare technology is required to do more than function properly. They also must protect sensitive information and create pleasant interactions with end users.

Outside of my technical background, my communications minor has strengthened my professional writing, interpersonal communication, and the ability to convey information to different audiences. These skills are valuable to have in professional settings where analysts converse with clinicians, administrators, and users who all have different levels of technical knowledge.

Having work experience during my undergraduate studies in customer service has led me to develop a unique set of skills. Staying calm in stressful interactions and adjusting my communication methods based on the individual situation are only a few. Serving as Academic Chair in the STEM based sorority, I was a part of strengthened my leadership and organization abilities. During the internship I had with JPMorgan Chase and work experience at Advent Health I was introduced to working within a large professional organization where I became comfortable working with different people and managing various responsibilities.

Rather than apply to law school with my undergraduate degree, I wish to have an in-depth education on healthcare technology via a graduate program prior to law school. I seek to deepen my understanding of healthcare technology in regard to data, privacy, security, and clinical systems as preparation for my specialized role as a lawyer within the sector. Health informatics specialists work with computerized healthcare systems and may assist with system design, implementation, problem solving, privacy, security, and user education (National Center for O*NET Development, 2026). Learning more about these areas will help me understand the real-world systems that healthcare laws and regulations are designed to govern.

Despite the always having an interest in law, the expansion of AI and lack of regulation surrounding such technology is what had me fully commit to it as a career. Post graduate school the goal is to attend law school to earn a Juris Doctor degree to pursue a career in health law. I plan to specialize in issue pertaining to digital health, health information privacy, artificial intelligence, and healthcare regulation and compliance. According to the American Bar Association health law is described to involve healthcare delivery, legislation and regulation, healthcare organizations, insurance, contracts, and compliance. (American Bar Association, n.d). The Occupational Outlook Handbook also explains that lawyers advise clients, interpret laws and regulations, conduct research, and communicate with clients and other professionals (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, 2026).

My career path is intentional. My undergraduate education gives me a foundation in technology and information systems, graduate study will deepen my understanding of healthcare technology and policy, and law school will prepare me to apply that background to legal and regulatory issues. Ultimately, I want to become a lawyer who understands healthcare from both a technical and human perspective. I want to use health law to help people who may struggle to understand or navigate the healthcare system, while also advocating for stronger patient protections and more equitable access to care. Over time, I hope to contribute to policy reform that makes the American healthcare system more accessible, transparent, secure, and responsive to patients.

Whether my work focuses on privacy, compliance, digital health regulation, patient advocacy, or policy development, I want it to have a meaningful impact. For me, health law offers the opportunity to combine technology, healthcare, communication, and advocacy in a way that supports individuals while contributing to broader improvements in the healthcare system.
